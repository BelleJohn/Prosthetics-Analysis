## Summary of Technologies
### 1. Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) and Neural Control
- Direct BCIs enable users to control prosthetic limbs using brain signals decoded from the motor cortex.
- Less invasive methods and improved signal-processing algorithms make this technology more accessible.

### 2. Muscle-Implantable Sensors
- Thin, flexible sensors implanted in residual muscles detect EMG signals for movement control.
- Startups like [Phantom Neuro](https://phantomneuro.com/) are pioneering this space for intuitive, surgery-free neural control.

### 3. Targeted Muscle Reinnervation (TMR)
- A surgical technique that reroutes residual nerves to remaining muscles.
- Creates new, intuitive control sites, enhancing signal clarity for prosthetic sensors.

### 4. AI-Driven Myoelectric Control
- Machine learning models adapt to user-specific muscle signal patterns.
- Enables smoother, more natural movements with reduced learning curves.

### 5. Haptic and Sensory Feedback
- Advanced systems provide pressure, texture, and temperature sensing through vibrations or electrical stimulation of the skin.
- Feedback is delivered to the nervous system, restoring a lifelike sense of touch and proprioception.

### 6. 3D Printing and Customization
- Enables cost-effective, rapid fabrication of customized prosthetic hands/arms. See [Open Bionics](https://openbionics.com/de/step-one/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=21234231019&gbraid=0AAAAABfVQW7MuEWWZu_GhMtJE8burOyON&gclid=CjwKCAjw56DBBhAkEiwAaFsG-p2PhqwHZfEB5S3XBSbtGEwpKtIgud0V3njhUzumkFPUsKhZa9glVxoCpwIQAvD_BwE).
- Improves comfort, accessibility, and personalization.

### 7. Modular, Multi-Articulated Devices
- Devices offer multiple powered joints and degrees of freedom.
- Allow for grip force feedback, simultaneous motions, and adaptive control.

## ✅ Pros and ❌ Cons of New Technologies

### ✅ Pros
- Highly improved control with AI and BCIs
- Sensory feedback improves interaction and safety
- Better customization and socket comfort via 3D printing
- Naturalistic, multi-joint motion in daily tasks

### ❌ Cons
- High cost remains a major barrier
- Complex training/fitting requirements for advanced devices
- Limited insurance coverage in many regions
- Socket discomfort and skin issues persist
- Uneven access to advanced tech by geography

## 🧾 Summary

The latest upper limb prostheses bring together **neural interfaces**, **AI-driven myoelectric control**, **haptic feedback**, and **rapid manufacturing** to push the boundaries of what’s possible in prosthetic function. 

- **OttoBock** leads in multi-articulating and pattern-recognition-based control.
- **Össur** offers intuitive and modular devices with a user-first approach.
- **Robobionics** emphasizes AI and accessibility.
- **DEKA/LUKE Arm** stands out for modularity and simultaneous movements.

The key trade-off is **function vs. accessibility**—with cost, training complexity, and real-world usability as ongoing challenges.
