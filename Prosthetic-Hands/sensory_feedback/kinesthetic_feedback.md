## 🦾 Non-Invasive Kinesthetic Feedback for Upper Limb Prostheses
This article explores non-invasive techniques for inducing kinesthetic (proprioceptive) sensations in upper limb prosthesis users. The goal is to enhance control, embodiment, and user satisfaction without requiring surgical procedures.

## 📌 Overview
Kinesthetic feedback—our sense of limb position and movement—is vital for natural, intuitive prosthesis control. Here investigates three main non-invasive methods to simulate such feedback:

## Key Techniques
| Method                    | Mechanism                     | Kinesthetic Sensation | Benefits                               | Limitations                       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Vibrotactile**          | Vibration motors on skin      | ✅ Yes                 | Simple; can encode grip force/position | May feel less natural/intuitive   |
| **Electrotactile (TENS)** | Electrical pulses on skin     | ✅ Yes                 | Finger-specific feedback; reduces pain | Skin impedance, bulky device size |
| **Skin-Stretch**          | Mechanical skin deformation   | ✅ Yes                 | Mimics movement directly               | Hardware complexity; less common  |


## 🔬 Findings & Challenges
- Electrotactile and vibrotactile feedback can improve agency and embodiment.
- Some studies show a reduction in phantom limb pain and improved prosthesis use.
- Functional improvements in daily life are not yet statistically significant.
- Major barriers: miniaturization, skin variability, user training, integration into real-world prosthetics.
